What exactly is a Quote?

This demo tests whether one canonical Quote model can represent the artisan examples while keeping publishing, duplication, explicit revisions, totals, options, exclusions, estimates, and VAT behaviour predictable without locking the Artisan’s work.

Proposed answer under test: a numbered Quote is always editable. The Artisan explicitly publishes its current contents as a preserved revision and Quote PDF. Later edits become unpublished changes; publishing again preserves the next revision. Duplication creates a new offer.

  • A Quote number is reserved when its first draft is created.
  • The working Quote always remains editable.
  • Publish Quote preserves revision 1; later Publish revision actions preserve revisions 2, 3, and so on.
  • Publishing without changes reuses the latest revision rather than creating a duplicate.
  • Every published Quote PDF shows its revision number.
  • Publishing implies the revision was prepared for sharing; sharing and Customer outcomes are not tracked.
  • A duplicate copies the visible working Quote into a new number but carries no revision history.
  • Price basis is explicit: fixed, approximate estimate, or time and materials.
  • Optional/excluded lines never silently enter the payable total.
  • Inclusion, certainty, work performer, and material supplier are independent line attributes.
  • Rich technical details and reference images belong to a scope line without changing its price shape.
  • Discounts and levies are named, ordered adjustments with an explicit pre- or post-VAT stage.
  • The MVP uses one currency and one VAT treatment/rate per Quote.
  • Published revisions preserve Customer, project, business, commercial terms, calculations, and VAT as they were at publication.
